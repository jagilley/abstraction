## Goal

1. **Build the second policy.** Can "what would I have forecast had that token been what I expected"
   be constructed in the model's own terms, and which construction is best?
2. **The prize per world, and the cost in the other.** With the two policies in hand, what does each
   buy after a structural edit, after a single-token glitch, and at a false alarm — and where, in the
   stream, is the damage a one-token repair would have to undo?
3. **The mixed world.** When a trigger may be any of the three, what is knowing which worth over the
   best fixed hedge, and can anything readable from the model earn it?
4. **What separates the worlds, and how fast.** The model's own quantities, the state, and the
   supervised ceiling, by offset after the flag.

Same substrate as the parent throughout; the two checkpoints are the clean-trained and ε-trained
models at 64k, the events are cut from the parent's `swap65k` stimuli, and the ε venue is
coeruleus's cached corrupted windows. Nothing here retrains anything.

## Instruments

- **The hold-and-discount forecast** ([`hold.py`](hold.py)). There is no mask token, so the held
  forecast is built by re-running the model with the flagged position overwritten. With `v = 16` the
  marginalisation over what the token *should* have been is exact at 16 forward passes, and four
  weightings fall out of the same passes: `argmax` (one pass), `topK`, `full` (the exact prior
  marginal) and **`pf`**, a particle filter in which the continuation reweights the imputed-token
  hypotheses as it arrives — causal at every offset, and the only weighting that uses the one piece of
  information that distinguishes a glitch from an edit. The `pf` weighting also hands over the
  model's own **log Bayes factor** between "that token was a glitch" and "that token was real", a gate
  with no fitted parameters.
- **The consumer.** A mixing weight `w` at each offset `τ` after the event,
  `q_w = (1 − w) q_native + w q_hold`; `w = 0` is reinterpret, `w = 1` is hold and discount.
  `−log q_w(x)` is convex in `w`, so the optimal `w` for any set of predictions is exact, and a gate →
  `w` map is the quantile step function [`coeruleus/gainloop.py`](../coeruleus/gainloop.py) used for
  its temperature, fitted on realised NLL with no oracle in it.
- **The two worlds, cut from one stream** ([`worlds.py`](worlds.py)). An `edit` event is a `swap65k`
  window flagged at its `t_v`. Its paired **glitch** is the *unedited* window with the token at the
  same `t_v` replaced — same original stream, same position, same `(k*, j)` stratum — either by a
  uniform draw (`glitch_m`, the ε process) or by the token nearest the edit's violator in model
  surprisal (`glitch_s`, since the uniform draw is 1.4–2.1 nats more surprising and would let a gate
  win on the flagged token alone). A `quiet` arm of disjoint unedited windows flagged at a random
  position is the cost of a false alarm. 6000 events per model from 51,637 eligible; `k*` histogram
  1113 / 959 / 1406 / 1364 / 1147 / 11 for `k* = 1…6`, `j` histogram 1517 / 1376 / 1346 / 1111 / 650
  for `j = 0…4`; mean `t_v − e` = 6.2, and 53% of flags fall beyond the end of the edited region. The
  consumer is event-triggered by assumption — detection is coeruleus Q2's question — so the base rate
  enters as the rate of quiet triggers.
- **The ε venue** ([`../coeruleus/events.py`](../coeruleus/events.py)): the isolated corruptions on the
  cached corrupted windows, scored against **both** references — column A, `p_L^ε`, the law for an
  observer of the window, which does not know the token was corrupted, with the ε family at the model's
  own `κ*` as ceiling; column B, the clean truth on the uncorrupted draw of the same windows, the law
  the continuation is actually drawn from once you are told the token was noise, with the clean family
  at `κ*_clean` as ceiling.
- **Self-checks.** The held forecast is bit-identical between a violator and its same-prefix legal
  twin (max |Δ| = 0.0), as it must be; the ε-window reconstruction matches the cached ladder;
  `κ*_ε = 4.85` and `κ*_clean = 4.85 / 4.90` reproduce `altitude/`; the column-A prizes 0.0387 / 0.5388
  reproduce coeruleus Q1's 0.038 / 0.538; the addendum's rebuilt populations match the main run's
  per-arm event surprisals at 0.0.

## Q1 — which imputation

Realised NLL under the pure hold policy, pooled over `τ = 0…8` (prior mass at the event on the edit
arm: top-1 0.627 / 0.673, top-4 0.926 / 0.955 for the ε- / clean-trained model):

| variant | ε-trained: edit / glitch / quiet | clean-trained: edit / glitch / quiet |
|---|---|---|
| `argmax` | 1.734 / 1.390 / 1.468 | 2.157 / 1.573 / 1.738 |
| `top4` | 1.594 / 1.265 / 1.316 | 1.830 / 1.284 / 1.362 |
| `full` | 1.544 / 1.252 / 1.291 | 1.705 / 1.258 / 1.308 |
| **`pf`** | **1.515 / 1.232 / 1.264** | **1.684 / 1.221 / 1.255** |

The particle filter wins on every arm of both models and is the instrument for everything below.

## Q2 — the prize per world, and the cost in the other

Realised NLL at the event (`τ = 0`), the exact per-offset optimal `w` (an in-hindsight minimiser,
stated as such), and the fraction of predictions on which the held forecast gave the realised token
more mass:

| arm | model | native | hold | `w*` | frac hold better | `H` native → hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| edit | ε-trained | 1.789 | 3.028 | **0.000** | 0.26 | 1.62 → 1.83 |
| edit | clean-trained | 2.502 | — | 0.228 | — | — |
| glitch (matched) | ε-trained | 2.908 | 1.744 | **0.999** | 0.74 | 1.61 → 1.78 |
| glitch (matched) | clean-trained | 6.282 | 1.738 | 0.999 | — | — |
| quiet | ε-trained | 1.164 | 1.924 | 0.000 | 0.14 | 1.23 → 1.97 |

**The two policies have opposite right answers on the same event.** On the glitch world holding is
worth 1.16 nats at the event on the ε-trained model and 4.54 on the clean-trained one, which has no
noise model at all; on the edit world the ε-trained model's optimal weight at the flag is exactly zero.
Holding at a false alarm costs 0.76 nats. Pooled over `τ = 0…8` in the mixed world at 1 : 1 : 1, pure
hold is worse than native on the ε-trained model (1.334 vs 1.299) and better on the clean-trained one
(1.384 vs 1.600), because the glitch-world prize there is so large.

**The edit's size is the knob.** Optimal `w` at the flag by the edit's size `j` (span `2^j` leaves):

| `w*(τ=0)` on edits | j=0 | j=1 | j=2 | j=3 | j=4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ε-trained | 0.151 | 0.001 | 0.002 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| clean-trained | 0.748 | 0.268 | 0.068 | 0.005 | 0.000 |

A `j = 0` swap is a single-token substitution and behaves as a glitch; from `j = 1` up holding is never
right on the ε-trained model. Monotone in both.

### Q2b — where the culprit is

Imputing at the flag buys nothing on edits; imputing `δ` tokens *before* it, at the true start of the
edited constituent (`delta_oracle`, mean `δ = 6.2`), buys +0.095 nats at the event on the ε-trained
model and +0.72 on the clean-trained one, and on the glitch arms the same lookback buys almost nothing
while `δ = 0` buys +1.07 / +4.35. By the edit's size, told the onset:

| `delta_oracle` on edits | j=0 | j=1 | j=2 | j=3 | j=4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ε-trained: `w*` / gain at `τ=0` | 0.875 / +0.126 | 0.527 / +0.222 | 0.439 / +0.086 | 0.181 / +0.003 | 0.064 / +0.000 |
| clean-trained: `w*` / gain | 0.985 / +2.096 | 0.544 / +0.886 | 0.309 / +0.122 | 0.129 / +0.006 | 0.000 / +0.000 |

> **The flag and the culprit are different positions, and the repair is one token wide.** Told where
> the damage starts, holding pays on one- and two-leaf edits and decays to nothing by `j = 3`, because
> a single-token imputation cannot undo a `2^j`-token edit. "Hold and discount" is a width-one repair;
> glitch and edit are the two ends of one axis, the width of the damaged span, and reinterpretation is
> the right policy from about `j = 2` up whether or not the culprit is known.**

### Q2c — the ε venue, against both references

| model | A: prize | A: hold recovers | B: prize | B: hold recovers | `H` native → hold at `τ=0` (`H(p_L^clean)` 1.147) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ε-trained 64k | 0.0387 | **−2.57** | 0.3247 | **0.608** | 1.580 → 1.968 |
| clean-trained 64k | 0.5388 | 0.628 | 0.9871 | 0.876 | 1.189 → 1.940 |

Against the window-observer's law (A) the ε-trained model's hold policy *loses* two and a half times
the prize, because that reference does not know the token was corrupted; against the law the
continuation is actually drawn from (B) it recovers 61%, and the clean-trained model 88%. Coeruleus's
temperature reached 0.4% of column A on the trained world; the replacement by a finer reading that
coeruleus Q1 said the missing nats lived in is what holding is, and this is its size. Holding also
manufactures the widening the model does not do on its own: `H` rises by 0.39 nats at the event, where the
model on its own moves it by hundredths (parent 2e).

### Q2d — the parent's 2e sign, and persistence, under each policy

Same-prefix pairs, ε-trained (501 pairs; clean-trained in brackets, 338). The violator and its legal
twin differ only at `t_v`, so the held forecast is identical between them and the paired statistic is
0.5 by construction at `w = 1` — an exact self-check on the whole imputation path:

| `w` | 0 | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| paired win rate, less confident after the violation | 0.423 (0.470) | 0.425 (0.462) | 0.421 (0.462) | 0.399 (0.473) | **0.500 (0.500)** |

The sign is not flipped by holding; it is **erased**, because under full hold the forecast never saw
the violator. The unmatched persistence profile (`dH` against the same window one step earlier; the
`quiet` arm reads +0.003 at `w = 0`) shows what each policy does to the model's stated uncertainty: at
the event, holding raises `dH` from +0.57 to +0.79 on edits and from +0.67 to +0.84 on glitches on the
ε-trained model, and from +0.003 to +0.745 on quiet — the widening a noise-aware Bayesian shows after a
glitch, now applied by the consumer wherever it is switched on.

## Q3 — the mixed world

A trigger fires; it is an edit, a glitch or nothing, at 1 : 1 : 1 (the maximum-entropy choice over a
trigger's provenance; the full grid over edit share 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 × false-alarm rate 0 / 0.33 / 0.5
is in the tables and every sign below holds on it). Every gated arm's map is fitted per offset on the
fit split and read out on held-out events; `label` is the exact world; every learned gate has a
shuffled twin (its values permuted across events, the `endogenous_teacher` control). Realised NLL:

| policy | ε-trained | clean-trained |
|---|---|---|
| native | 1.2986 | 1.6004 |
| hold | 1.3341 | 1.3835 |
| **best fixed per-offset `w`** | **1.2640** | **1.3015** |
| exact label | 1.2316 | 1.2642 |
| oracle per prediction | 1.1996 | 1.1970 |

**The arbitration prize is small.** Knowing the world exactly is worth 0.032 (ε) / 0.037 (clean) nats
per event over the best fixed hedge, against per-world prizes near a nat: a fixed intermediate weight
already captures most of the value, and arbitration is cheap to get roughly right and expensive to get
exactly right. Each gate against its own shuffle, share of the label prize:

| gate | ε-trained | clean-trained |
|---|---|---|
| the model's own log Bayes factor (`logbf`) | **+0.09** | **+0.26** |
| running excess since the event | +0.07 | +0.24 |
| the banked coeruleus excess head | −0.01 | +0.14 |
| the model's entropy | +0.00 | +0.02 |
| supervised world probe on the residual stream | **−0.20** | **−0.16** |

Every shuffle lands within 0.001 of the best fixed policy, which is the control working. The only
gate that beats its shuffle robustly on both models is the model's own likelihood ratio between the
two hypotheses, a by-product of the second forward pass the consumer already runs. The supervised
probe on the state is worse than gating on nothing.

## Q4 — what separates the worlds, and how fast

AUC(edit vs glitch) by offset after the flag, on the surprisal-matched guard (n = 1566 / 1695, guard
0.499 / 0.500); below 0.5 means the gate reads higher on the glitch, and the distance from 0.5 is the
separation:

| gate, ε-trained | τ=0 | τ=1 | τ=2 | τ=4 | τ=8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **supervised state probe** | **0.712** | **0.786** | 0.777 | 0.719 | 0.657 |
| `logbf` | 0.499 | **0.242** | 0.212 | 0.201 | 0.196 |
| running excess | 0.491 | 0.361 | 0.363 | 0.378 | 0.396 |
| banked excess heads | 0.46–0.52 | 0.46–0.51 | 0.48–0.49 | 0.49–0.50 | 0.50 |
| `post_embed` / random-trunk floors | 0.57–0.58 | 0.51–0.52 | 0.50–0.51 | 0.50–0.51 | 0.50–0.51 |

The model's own quantities are at chance at the flag — the log Bayes factor *equals* the flagged
token's surprisal there, by construction — and separate the worlds at 0.76–0.78 one token later (0.91 on structural edits). One
token of continuation is all it takes. Against `quiet`, surprisal, `logbf` and the running excess all
read 0.88–0.91 at every offset: noticing *that* something happened is easy; the question is which.

## Addendum (2026-09-17) — six controls on the gate side

The state probe's early lead and its failure as a gate had mundane candidate causes; each was ruled
in or out ([`gates2.py`](gates2.py), same populations and split as the main run, asserted).

- **A1. Structural edits only** (`j ≥ 2`, n = 3107). The probe separates the worlds *better* (0.855 at
  `τ=0`, 0.923 at `τ=1`) and gates *worse* (+0.009 vs its shuffle, from +0.006). The arbitration prize
  grows (0.032 → 0.042 ε, 0.037 → 0.050 clean); every other gate keeps its sign and size. It was not
  the label.
- **A2–A3. The target and the missing class.** A probe trained on where holding pays (`b > a`) is the
  best readout of that label (0.67–0.70 at `τ=0`) and the worst gate in the table (+0.020 to +0.057 vs
  its shuffle). A three-class probe identifies false alarms (p(quiet) 0.80 on quiet) and still sets its
  highest `w` on quiet, because a one-dimensional gate `log p(glitch) − log p(edit)` reads quiet as
  "not an edit". It was neither the target nor the class coverage.
- **A4. The preamble.** The world probe read on the state at `t_v − 1`, *before* the flagged token:
  0.715 matched, against the same probe's 0.728 at `τ = 0`; exactly 0.500 where the edit starts at the
  flag and the prefixes are identical (n = 467, states equal to machine precision). The parent's 2b
  scalar — the model's mean surprisal over the four predictions already made — reads 0.691 there, the
  `post_embed` floor 0.528. **The state's early lead is the edited preamble**, the tonic signal
  `altitude/` Q4a showed belongs to the stimulus, and a scalar integrator of the model's own recent
  surprisal has essentially all of it.
- **A5. Sign versus size versus level transfer.** Rank correlation with the signed payoff
  `log(b/a)` and AUC on its sign do not predict gate value; the *binned out-of-sample `R²`* on the
  payoff — the gate's own step function pointed at the stake — does, in every cell. The payoff probe
  has the best rank correlation and the best sign-AUC and the worst binned `R²`. A fitted probe's scores
  on its own fit rows are optimistic, so the bin → level map learned there fails out of sample even
  where the ordering survives. That is a third form of "a detector's AUC is not enough", beside
  coeruleus's base-rate form and the sign-versus-size form.
- **A6. A readout trained on the stake itself.** Closed-form ridge on the state predicting `log(b/a)`,
  label-free. Held-out `R²` 0.100 / 0.132 (ε, all / `j ≥ 2`) and 0.106 / 0.099 (clean) at `τ = 0`,
  0.017–0.161 at `τ = 1`, ~0 by `τ = 3` on the ε-trained model and ~0.03 out to `τ = 8` on the clean
  one; the log Bayes factor rescaled on the same rows reads 0.003–0.028 at the event and keeps a small
  rank correlation (0.06–0.19) at every offset instead. As a gate the ridge beats its shuffle in **all
  eight cells** (−0.001 to −0.006 nats, 1–14% of the arbitration prize), the only state readout in the
  round that does; an MLP on the same target predicts better at `τ = 0–1` and loses as a gate
  everywhere (its binned `R²` goes to −0.18 and −0.38 at `τ = 1`), coeruleus's closed-form gotcha on
  the decision side.

> **The state separates the worlds early and well, and that is the stimulus. What the state knows
> about the decision — how much holding will pay — is about a tenth of the variance at the flagged
> token and essentially nothing after it, cashable by a closed-form linear readout for about a
> hundredth of a nat. The arbiter is buildable in principle and worth little on this substrate.**

## What this establishes, and what it does not

**Establishes** (one grammar, one architecture, the clean- and ε-trained 64k checkpoints, one
stimulus family, one noise dose):

- The hold-and-discount consumer is buildable exactly in the model's own terms, and a particle filter
  over the imputed token is its best form. It is worth 61–88% of the glitch-world prize against the
  reference that knows the token was noise, where coeruleus's temperature reached 0.4% on the trained
  world; the "replacement by a finer reading" coeruleus Q1 located the missing nats in is this policy.
- Reinterpretation is the right policy for structural edits, now by an arm: the optimal hold weight is
  zero at the flag on the ε-trained model for every `j ≥ 1`, and told the true onset it decays to zero
  by `j = 3`. Glitch and edit are the ends of one axis, the width of the damaged span; the consumer is
  a width-one repair.
- Under full hold the parent's 2e statistic is 0.5 by construction, and the consumer manufactures
  the widening a noise-aware Bayesian shows.
- The two worlds are the same event at the token and diverge in the continuation: the model's own
  log Bayes factor is at chance at the flag and separates the worlds at ~0.8 one token later, and is
  the only fitted-parameter-free gate that beats its shuffle on both models.
- The state's separation of the worlds at and before the flag is the parent's tonic signal, a
  stimulus property carried almost entirely by a scalar integrator of recent surprisal.
- Knowing the world is worth 0.03–0.05 nats per event over the best fixed hedge. Classifier-shaped
  state probes lose to their shuffles whatever their label, target or class coverage, for a reason
  that is now pinned (level transfer); a closed-form ridge on the stake is the one state readout that
  gates, for 1–14% of that prize.

**Does not establish:**

- Anything about finding the trigger. Every event here is handed to the consumer; detection at a low
  base rate is coeruleus Q2's question and its answer stands.
- A consumer that repairs spans wider than one token. The natural extension imputes a constituent
  rather than a token; the lookback arm bounds where it would pay (`j ≤ 2`) and where it would not.
- Whether an arbiter trained *jointly* with the consumer, rather than a fitted map on a fixed gate,
  changes the picture; every gate here was fitted after the fact.
- The mixture. 1 : 1 : 1 is a choice; the grid is shown and every sign holds on it, but the prize's
  size moves with the rates.
- Transfer off `v16 s2 L6 m4`, off this architecture, or to a corruption model other than single-token
  glitches and legally-regrown edits.

## Gotchas worth not rediscovering

- **Cut the two worlds from one stream.** The glitch has to be the unedited window at the same
  position and stratum as its edit, or a gate wins on the flagged token's surprisal alone; and even
  then, match the glitch token's surprisal to the violator's (`glitch_s`), since a uniform draw is
  1.4–2.1 nats more surprising.
- **The flag is not the culprit.** Mean `t_v − e` is 6.2 tokens and 53% of flags fall beyond the edited
  region. Impute at the flag and you repair the wrong token on every edit wider than one leaf.
- **Two references in the ε venue disagree in sign.** The window-observer's law and the clean law the
  continuation is drawn from give opposite verdicts on the same policy for the ε-trained model. Report
  both and say which is which (coeruleus's gotcha, now with both columns computed).
- **A probe's lead at the flag needs the `t_v − 1` control.** If the state separates the worlds at
  `τ = 0` it may be reading the preamble; the parent's tonic scalar is the comparison.
- **A gate's AUC is not enough, third form.** Pre-screen a gate by the binned out-of-sample `R²` of its
  own step function on the stake, not by AUC or rank correlation; fit any stake readout in closed
  form; expect a fitted classifier's levels not to transfer.
- **A sibling node writing its launch log breaks the image build.** Give the Modal image an `ignore`
  that skips `results/` trees and `.log` files, and write launch logs outside the tree.
