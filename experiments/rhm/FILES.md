Entanglement watch clean (fresh FM stays +0.15 > co-trained — no privatization). See RHM_SCULPT_CONTINUAL_README |
| `rhm_sculpt_lengthgen.py` | Sculpting length-generalization × forward-model ARITY × closed-vs-open loop (motivated by reading/human-vision.pdf, the FoveAgent length-gen paper): trains the Stage-3b apparatus once at the short range (c≤train_corrupt), frozen, then sweeps plan length `c` and beam width with token / {arity-2, arity-1} × {closed re-grounded, open imagined-rollout} latent beams, plus a budget-slack sweep at fixed c. Owns `_build_block_fm_arity1` (command-blind FM, MSE-optimum = E_k[Δz]), `_latent_beam(open_loop=…)` (imagined rollout + move-sequence tracking), `_eval_at`. Findings: (1) coordination/beam-width is the length-gen variable (myopic breaks OOD, wide holds); (2) arity is load-bearing only at greedy / when search can't substitute — it washes out at wide beam under the closed loop (generator confound; not recovered by tightening the budget); (3) FINALIZER — under open-loop imagined rollout, width *amplifies* arity instead of substituting for it (a2≫a1 at every width, 2.5–3.6× for c≤3), because a move-blind FM's imagined trajectory is identical across moves. The DOF's irreplaceable function is counterfactual simulation; horizon-capped by the one-step-FM composition limit. See RHM_SCULPTING_README "Does the forward model's arity predict length-generalization?" |
| `rhm_sculpt_twofm.py` | ONE forward model vs TWO (cerebellum + hippocampus), motivated by the hippocampal forward-sweep / VTE discussion. Answers the length-gen Round-3 boundary ("open-loop imagined rollout drifts past ~6 steps; needs multi-step-consistency FM training to extend"). Single controlled variable = which FM rolls the OPEN loop: a one-step "cerebellar" FM (`_train_block_fm`) vs a new multi-step "hippocampal" FM (`_train_block_fm_multistep` — unrolled H≤train_budget steps fed its OWN predictions, grad through the composition; at H=1 reduces to the one-step loss, so H is the only knob). Trained on IDENTICAL data; everything else shared/frozen. Three composed conditions (ONE-FM / TWO-FM / MULTISTEP-ONLY) from 6 beam lines (token, {closed,open}×{1step,multistep}, open_a1 floor) over a length sweep; plus `_rollout_fidelity` drift curves and `_fm_check` one-step currency. Findings: (1) the multi-step FM extends the open-loop horizon *incrementally* — >open_1step in all 24 cells, width-gated + horizon-amplified, but recovers only ~14% of the open→closed imagination penalty (a sharpening, not the arity floor→capable step-change); (2) the two-organ SEPARATION is not load-bearing — the multi-step FM costs nothing one-step (matched closed-loop beams + ranking currency), so it strictly dominates and a single multi-step FM serves both loops (the multi-step loss is a superset). Verdict: filed, not adopted (incremental, open-loop-only; task never forces open-loop). See RHM_SCULPTING_README "One forward model vs two". |
| `rhm_sculpt_internalize.py` | Sculpting Internalization (the REACHING_INTERNAL port): does closing the planning loop reshape the *belief* itself? Nested 3-rung ladder on the parser belief, single controlled variable = whether/how-much the loop touches the belief during training — `frozen` (root-CE only, = Stage-3b parser) ⊂ `fm_cotrain` (+ a2a asymmetric FM local-loss into the belief: endogenous "be predictable") ⊂ `planner` (+ differentiable grounded planner CE `value(z+FM(z,k))` imitating the DP best-move `k*`: int_plan port). Identical frozen-downstream eval per rung (depth-probe + PR, fresh MC value, fresh block FM, `_fm_check`, token+latent beams) so the fresh-FM latent beam == the fresh-external-planner transferability test. Two headlines: (A) belief becomes plannable (FM top1/rank-corr↑, clean-channel gap↑), (B) frontier-moving vs capped (belief-depth d3↑ under grounded rung, capped under endogenous rung — the RHM_LATENT_LOOP mlm-vs-data2vec unification, now on the planning side). Owns `_collect_grounded_moves` (controller-independent DP best-move buffer), `_train_fm_cotrain`, `_train_planner_internal`, `_downstream_eval` |
| `specialization/rhm_subtree_specialization.py` | Specialization bucket, Exp 1: FULL vs SUBTREE breadth control — does restricting training to a root-subtree (matched token budget, identical init) buy depth on that subtree's own domain? Self-contained (own root-restricted trace generator + per-level probe). Finding: **inert** — SUBTREE ≈ FULL at every level (Δ≤0.013), and doesn't even forget the complement (shared low-level rules transfer). The scaling-era "specialize-doesn't-buy-depth" claim on a controlled DGP. See specialization/README.md |
| `specialization/rhm_spec_direct_target.py` | Specialization bucket, Cut-2a: does concentrating a *direct* deep target (oracle ancestor CE) onto subtree A buy selective/advantaged depth? Conditions `ntp`/`aux_all`/`aux_A`/`ntp_A` all share flat-NTP-on-all-roots; single controlled variable = the A-concentrated term; swept m∈{4,8,12}; per-subtree (A/complement/full) probing. Self-contained (copies the aligned oracle-aux machinery + trace generators + probe). Finding: **concentration never helps — it *hurts* the target subtree, worst at the capacity-limited m=8 (aux_A−aux_all d4 −0.14), and can't make the complement shallow (A-only supervision lifts B's d4 0.11→0.41 via shared rules)**. The Student-B>Student-A result: broad beats narrow at A's own depth. First RHM-vs-language disanalogy → motivates a partially-heterogeneous DGP (cut-3). See specialization/README.md |
