# A history of the RHM experiment (2026-06-21 → present)

*Scope note: this traces the intellectual arc of `experiments/rhm/` specifically — one directory in a larger monorepo — reconstructed from ~34 commits and the ~30 auxiliary READMEs they left behind. It is not a day-by-day log (the git messages themselves are useless: "yeah", "stuff!", "qwerty" — all the real content lives in the docs). It is an attempt to trace *why* the questions changed shape over a month of work, and what that says about how this research program actually updates its priors.*

## Why RHM exists at all

The project didn't start here. It started in `experiments/language_reduction/`, doing scaling-law interventions on natural language, and ran into a wall: vocabulary reduction turned out to be a *channel* intervention (it changes β, the scaling offset) rather than a *data-generating-process* intervention (which would change γ, the scaling exponent's shape) — and worse, the spectral-denoising machinery used to measure this was entangled with the very statistics it was trying to isolate. Natural language doesn't let you cleanly change the thing you want to change.

The Random Hierarchy Model (Cagnetta & Wyart, 2024) was adopted as the fix: a synthetic language with a literal, known, adjustable generative process — a tree of composition rules, `L` levels deep, each feature expandable `m` synonymous ways into `s` children over a `v`-symbol vocabulary. Every design choice in everything that follows traces back to this one motivating property: **you can look up the ground truth**. Every subsequent arc in this history is, in some sense, an exploitation of that transparency to answer a question that would be unanswerable — or answerable only equivocally — on real data.

## Era 1 — scaling laws and the first forward-model probes (06-21 → 06-22)

The opening moves were the straightforward ones: sweep `L` and `m`, fit scaling exponents, and find that **`m` dominates `L` roughly 3:1** — synonymic multiplicity (per-level ambiguity), not hierarchical depth, is the scaling bottleneck. Then the project imported its sibling machinery from `a2a_forward/` — a forward model (FM) trained to predict a later layer's activations from an earlier layer's — and asked whether the FM's residual (what it fails to predict) reflects the DGP's complexity. The early answer was a caution rather than a result: at the tiny scale first tried, the FM predicted 99%+ of the computation regardless of setting, and the regime-transition experiment was explicitly logged as **"not testable at this scale."** This turns out, in retrospect, to be the first sighting of a problem that would take a month to properly diagnose: *most of the DGP settings this project tried in its first three weeks were simply too easy or too hard for anything interesting to be visible in the residual.* Getting the regime right — not any particular technique — is what unlocks nearly everything from late June onward, and (see Era 8) it turns out to be a lesson the project would need to re-derive and defend against, not just once but as a standing methodological reflex, a month later.

A parallel thread tried to fix the "models only learn 1-2 levels of composition" pattern by re-engineering the loss: label smoothing, focal loss, confidence thresholding, FM-surprise-weighted NTP. The verdict, arrived at cleanly and never revisited: **the composition-depth ceiling is a capacity/signal constraint, not an artifact of cross-entropy's sharpening bias.** Softening the objective doesn't change which levels get learned — but gradient *reallocation* (focal loss, confidence thresholds) does make the resulting computation more legible to a forward model, a minor finding that quietly seeds the much bigger "objective, not architecture, is the lever" theme that dominates the second half of this history.

## Era 2 — the ratchet arc, and its clean, informative negative (06-23 → 06-29)

The a2a sibling project had found something exciting on MNIST: a "wake-sleep ratchet" — inject a forward-model forecast, distill, re-initialize the FM, repeat — that *compounds*, widening the val-loss gap 34%→48% over four cycles. The obvious next question was whether the same loop compounds on RHM, where (unlike MNIST) the ground-truth hierarchy lets you decompose *exactly what* is or isn't improving, level by level.

This became the project's first sustained arc, and it is a model of how to run a negative result properly. Nine separate experiments (unified-gate ratchet, m=2 control, sparsity sweep, sparse ratchet, overparameterization control, RL-based ratchet, generation-based distillation, FOMAML diagnostic, five-way meta-learning sweep) systematically ruled out one candidate explanation after another: not capacity, not task structure (RL is sparse-but-rich like classification, so that's not it either), not the distillation bottleneck (generation-based distillation restores NTP performance and it still doesn't compound), not FM-cosine regime (the sweet spot is reachable and it still doesn't compound). **The ratchet reproduces every MNIST *dynamic* — gate closing, FM tracking, robustness dissociation, crossover timing — but never the *magnitude*.** The FOMAML diagnostic delivered the mechanistic verdict: on stationary data, no outer bilevel objective makes FM-predictability pressure improve compositional learning, because "the outer loop is identical to the inner loop." The arc's own retrospective converged on a single sentence that recurs, independently rediscovered, at least twice more over the following weeks: **compounding requires a moving frontier, and stationary RHM doesn't have one.**

This is the first instance of a pattern that becomes the defining rhythm of the whole project: *try to reproduce a positive result from the sibling project or from the literature; watch it fail on RHM in a specific, diagnosable way; treat that specific failure mode as the actual finding.* RHM's job in this research program is less "a scaling-law testbed" and more **a differential-diagnosis instrument** — a place to send a phenomenon that works somewhere else but for reasons nobody has pinned down, because RHM's transparency turns "it doesn't replicate" into "here is exactly what property the other domain had that this one lacks."

## Era 3 — going inward: what is actually learnable, and why "1-2 levels" was partly a mirage (06-29)

Rather than continue testing the ratchet at ever-more-refined settings, the project stepped back from parameter-space entirely and asked, training-free: is the deep compositional structure even *recoverable* from the leaves? *Representable* by the architecture in use? The **deep composition arc** answered both questions cleanly — recoverable (exact belief propagation gives a real ceiling), representable (a model given the true latents as an auxiliary signal reaches that ceiling at every level, root included) — and then discovered that no local self-supervised objective anyone could build reached deep composition on its own: not next-token prediction, not masked-span prediction of any masking geometry, not four different contrastive/DINO/Sinkhorn invariance constructions. **The high-level signal is intrinsically diluted in the data**; every local task inherits that dilution because "infer a deep subtree's identity from context" *is* the diluted long-range inference, however the loss is dressed up.

This did more than answer its own question — it **recontextualized the project's entire prior three weeks.** The recurring "models learn only 1-2 levels and plateau" observation (per-level loss, the ratchet, the meta-learning nulls) had almost always been measured at `v=8, m=4` (occupancy 0.50), a setting where even a Bayes-optimal classifier recovers the root only ~40% of the time. A meaningful fraction of every prior "plateau" was **the information ceiling, not an optimization failure** — the project had spent three weeks partly measuring an artifact of its own default hyperparameters. This is the month's first genuinely mid-course update to a standing belief, and it licenses everything that follows: from here on, DGP regime (m, occupancy) is treated as a first-class experimental variable, not a fixed default.

## Era 4 — the theoretical pivot: targets, not tokens (07-01 → 07-03)

The frontier-and-legibility experiments sharpened deep composition's finding into something more precise: **synonymic multiplicity `m`, not tuple-space occupancy, gates the learnable frontier's *depth*; occupancy only gates its *quality and ceiling*.** At `m=2` — for the first time — plain next-token prediction reaches the root, and on that substrate the FM residual becomes hierarchy-legible in exactly the MNIST-like way the ratchet arc had never seen. This explained, retroactively, the entire prior RHM self-knowledge silence: **every earlier closed-loop attempt had run at `m≥4`, where the deep residual is structureless — there was nothing for a forward model to amplify.** The FM-as-regularizer experiment then made the "amplifier, not source" claim concrete in a different register: structured FM-predictability pressure compresses the functional circuit below weight decay's floor, but self-knowledge (the loop) is *not* needed for that — the simplest open-loop pressure suffices. And the complexodynamics reading (via Aaronson's "First Law") added a durable refinement to the whole compression story: without an explicit annealer, SGD alone "freezes mid-descent, a glass, not a liquid," and the residual's descent — where it does happen — doesn't go to zero but to a floor set by the DGP's own irreducible sophistication.

The real pivot of this era, though, arrived from outside the project's own experiments. The sleep-chunking line — asking whether a generic capacity bottleneck implements motor-style "chunking" — found instead that a *reconstruction* objective does the opposite of chunking (it keeps high-variance surface detail, sheds the abstraction) while an *external-role* objective (predict the neighbor, not yourself) produces clean top-down chunking. Trying to make that chunking *recursive* (chunk the chunks) surfaced a structural fact: the recursion needs a parent one level up, and it stalled exactly at the model's own inference frontier — **dilution, not cascading error, was the wall.** Chasing down *why* led to a paper: Korchinski, Favero & Wyart (RHM's own originators), "Learn from your own latents and not from tokens." Its sample-complexity result — token objectives cost exponential-in-depth `vm^(ℓ+2)` to learn level `ℓ`; learning from your own *lifted latents* costs a depth-independent `vm³` — is not an analogy but an exact description of the wall this project had been hitting for two weeks, under many different names (the composition ceiling, the ratchet's stall, the recursive-chunking frontier). A controlled experiment confirmed the direction (latent-target recursion beats token-target recursion at every deep level, gap growing with depth) though not yet the magnitude (the minimal operator built here is weaker than proper clustering).

From this point on, **"what you train a model to predict, not how you architect it," becomes the project's dominant lever**, applied over and over in the arcs that follow — and the shift is explicit enough that it gets a name in the docs: the target/input distinction ("feeding a latent in does nothing if you predict tokens out").

## Era 5 — self-knowledge appears, and is dissolved into its parts (07-04 → 07-08)

The **latent-loop** experiment is the direct payoff of Era 4's pivot, and arguably the single most important result in the project's history. Closing the cerebellar wake-sleep loop on RHM produces **generalizable self-knowledge for the first time** — but only under a latent (own-lifted-DGP) target, never under a token target at any depth or loop strength. The mechanism, established with real rigor (a falsified prediction included — a deep token-residual at `m=2` turned out *not* to be sufficient): the FM residual is a map of the model's computational frontier; a token target structures it only where the model already reaches; a latent target moves the frontier to the root, making the residual FM-invariant (any independently-trained forward model leaves the same residual) — and it is that FM-invariance, not residual depth per se, that self-knowledge requires.

The project then did something unusual for an already-positive result: it took the result apart. A meta/object decomposition (ported from the sibling MNIST work) showed the entire self-knowledge signal is **meta-knowledge** ("where my self-model errs"), not object-level absorption of the FM's function — object-level knowledge stayed flat and un-acquired everywhere. And a follow-up sparse-supervision test found that **distillation transfers nothing at all on RHM**, for a reason worth keeping: RHM's DGP is a compact, shared grammar, so 5% of labeled positions already lets a student re-derive the whole thing — there is no surplus for a teacher to transfer. This is presented explicitly as a *feature*, not a gap: it is the clean control proving a regime where distillation is structurally vacuous, sharpening (by contrast) how uncertain the field's belief in MNIST/language distillation-as-transfer actually is, since neither of those domains has ever run the equivalent control.

## Era 6 — RHM becomes a control task (07-10 → 07-15)

Having found where self-knowledge lives, the project turned to a second sibling-project transplant: the a2a "reaching" arc's finding that *internalizing* a forward self-model (co-training it, letting the loop reshape the operator) makes an operator dramatically more plannable. Porting this required first giving RHM something to plan.

**Active RHM** tried the natural port — an agent choosing which masked blocks to reveal — and got a clean structural null: a mean-Δ forward model cannot plan an *epistemic* action, because the value of information lives in the *variance* of the belief update across a query's unknown contents, which a point estimate discards by construction. Predicting the decision variable directly (expected posterior entropy — a value-of-information head) fixed it at `m=2` and gave a genuinely new instrument: a computable, in-advance diagnostic (per-instance query-rank correlation) for whether a setting is plannable at all. Internalization itself, though, turned out to have **no headroom** here: active-query RHM is "inference in disguise" — its only state is the belief over the hidden root, so *acting* well already requires computing the same thing *planning* would forecast (act ≈ plan). Reaching's internalization worked because control genuinely separates a policy from a world model that a reflex can shortcut between; querying has no such separation to exploit.

That diagnosis pointed straight at the fix: make RHM a genuine control task, where an agent *edits* tokens rather than merely observes them. **Edit control** confirmed the arity result reverses cleanly (a mean-Δ FM is now a decisive planner, because an edit's consequence is deterministic-given-the-state rather than variance-in-hidden-content) but surfaced a second, independent obstacle nobody had anticipated: the belief that judges success is a perfect parser *on-manifold* but **completely gameable off-manifold**, because editing forces excursions through the "word-salad" states between a corrupted sequence and a repaired one. A generator-constrained action space plus a self-consistency veto (the generator's own surprise as an on-manifold check — cortex proposes, cerebellum calibrates) converted near-total gaming (true success ≈0) into majority genuine control (≈0.65).

**Sculpting** is where this line matured into the project's most extensive single arc — five stages plus two side-quests, all built around one question: does planning in the model's own latents (rather than materializing every candidate move as tokens and re-encoding) actually help? The path there is itself instructive: an initial attempt collapsed for a reason that had nothing to do with latents — the task (corrupt-and-repair) turned out to be **greedy-decomposable** (a perfect one-step ranker solves it), so there was no lookahead prize for any planner, latent or not, to capture. Only after building a combinatorial pre-check that certified a genuine coordination prize (a DP optimum a myopic reflex provably cannot reach, tunable by depth and coupling tightness) did a learned beam search show the expected monotone lookahead benefit. With that task now legitimate, latent-space planning turned out to be an *efficient surrogate* on the clean channel (92% of token-space performance at ~8x lower materialization cost) — but genuinely *superior*, not just cheaper, the moment the channel becomes lossy (partial observability or stochastic dynamics), because a latent belief carries information a token round-trip destroys or forces a single noisy sample of. The belief-quality axis then closed a loop back to Era 5: a deeper belief makes the forward model a better one-step ranker, and — the more striking result — a **non-privileged**, self-supervised masked-infilling belief beats even the privileged, ground-truth-labeled oracle as a planning substrate, because it happens to transfer better to the off-manifold states a planner actually visits. And internalizing the *planning loop itself* onto the belief reproduced Era 5's frontier-moving-vs-capped dichotomy exactly, on a new axis: an endogenous "be-predictable" pressure caps, while a *grounded* planning objective (imitating a DP-computed best move) moves the frontier and expands the belief's effective dimensionality.

Two threads try to make this compound. **Continual internalization** iterates the whole planning loop and finds a **weak ratchet**: value-iteration is a large one-round compounding lever, one-shot belief internalization is a large ceiling-setter, but continuous re-internalization adds almost nothing — the belief frontier exhausts in a single pass. This is the ratchet arc's core finding (Era 2), and RHM_LATENT_LOOP's "two novelties" observation (Era 5), independently reproduced a third time, now inside the planning domain, with the cleanest mechanistic account yet: on a *fixed* task, there is simply nothing left to climb after the first internalization pass. **Length-generalization** asks whether the forward model's extra degree of freedom (arity — conditioning on the action, not just the state) is what lets planning generalize to longer horizons, echoing a recent vision paper on locality and length generalization. The answer sharpens the paper's claim into something more general and falsifiable: search width is the real length-generalization variable, and the forward model's extra degree of freedom is only *load-bearing* when you cannot cheaply act-and-observe — in a closed loop, search substitutes for a good forecast; in an open loop (planning by imagination, no re-grounding), it cannot, and the arity gap becomes large and width-amplified. A follow-up ("one forward model vs two," motivated by the cerebellum/hippocampus distinction in neuroscience) found that a *single* multi-step-consistency-trained forward model serves both the closed and open loop equally well — the biological two-organ separation is not structurally necessary here; the training *objective*, not the *architecture*, is again the whole story.

## Era 7 — specialization, and a reframe that revalues its own "failure" (07-16 → 07-18)

The last completed arc asked whether the depth frontier can be moved not by a better target but by **allocation** — concentrating training on a subtree, or reweighting the loss toward deep tokens. Both are dead ends, and cleanly so: breadth-restriction is inert (a model trained only on a quarter of the tree learns that quarter's deep structure no better than a model trained on everything), and level-reweighting is actively harmful (deep composition is built *on top of* the shallow substrate, so starving low levels of gradient removes the foundation deep levels compose from). Only a *direct* deep target — a real label, not a reweighted diluted one — recruits depth, reconfirming Era 4's target/input distinction from a new angle.

The sharper test concentrated even that *direct* target onto a subtree, expecting a domain-specific advantage at high synonymity. It found the opposite: broad training beats narrow on the *narrow* domain's own held-out data (the "Student-B > Student-A" result), because RHM's single shared ruleset means every subtree's deep structure is computed by the same rules as everything else's — there is no domain-specific deep structure to specialize on. This was first logged as the arc's headline negative and the first sharp RHM-vs-language disanalogy, motivating a plan to bolt genuine heterogeneity onto the DGP.

Then, within 48 hours, a discussion inverted the reading entirely — and this reversal is worth dwelling on, because it is the cleanest example in the whole history of the project catching its own framing bias in real time. **"Broad beats narrow" isn't a deficiency to engineer around — it is the enabling condition for sample-efficient generalization**, and the interesting question was never "why can't we specialize" but "what does a human learner have that lets it climb from a subtree with no deep label, when a fixed-capacity NTP learner provably cannot even with abundant broad data." The candidate mechanism floated — an intrinsic learning-progress signal that reallocates capacity once a level is consolidated — is explicitly *not yet tested*; the reframe is a standing hypothesis, not a result. But it retroactively re-reads three weeks of "frontier stalls, ratchet doesn't compound, specialization is inert" findings as facets of one open question: **what would make RHM's frontier move on its own**, without more data or privileged labels, the way a human's evidently does.

## Era 8 — the confabulation test: turning the toolkit on itself (07-22 → 07-23)

The last completed arc is also the most reflexive: it asks not whether a model can plan or specialize, but whether a *self-report* it produces is really about itself. The standing objection to any introspection claim (Nisbett & Wilson, cited directly in the design doc, [`ideas/confabulation_test.md`](../../ideas/confabulation_test.md)) is that a report may come from a learned *theory* of oneself rather than genuine access. The FM-residual decomposition this project had spent a month building — `a_j = FM(a_i) + r` — gives that objection an operational form for the first time: `FM(a_i)` is, by construction, everything a self-theory could produce from observing the model, so **confabulation lives in the range of the self-model, and whatever cannot be confabulated lives in the residual.** Full writeup and results: [`confabulation/README.md`](confabulation/README.md).

Built on the one substrate known (from Era 5) to carry generalizable self-knowledge — the latent-target `ntp_aux{,_cl}` recipe, forked verbatim and validated to reproduce its reference val loss to three decimal places — a report head is trained to report either the FM-residual's direction (**IMPL**) or three control quantities computable from the input–output map alone (own-**BEHAV**ior, output-**ENT**ropy, ground-truth **WORLD**-state). Against a battery of capacity-matched third-party observers, **IMPL is the only target where the self-report has a first-person advantage** (+0.09 to +0.11): BEHAV is a dead null, and on ENT and WORLD the third party actually *wins* — exactly what the "not cheaply recoverable from the I/O map" criterion predicts. The sharpest secondary result is that the **observer ladder is flat in capacity**: a third-party model the same size as the subject itself does no better at guessing IMPL than a tiny one, while the same ladder climbs steeply on the input-determined WORLD target — the observer is *access*-limited, not resource-limited, which is a cleaner and more falsifiable statement of "the third party genuinely lacks information" than a single-model comparison could ever give.

Two features of this arc connect it directly to the project's oldest and newest lessons. First, it makes Era 1's founding caution — that an over-capacity forward model manufactures a degenerate residual, and every measurement built on that residual becomes measurement of noise — into an explicit, load-bearing control rather than a scar from early failure: a smoke run deliberately re-created the exact "not testable at this scale" regime (FM cosine 0.994, the same number the 06-21 regime-transition experiment hit) and it manufactured the *entire* headline effect from junk (+0.118 "introspective advantage," 2.07× steering ratio, from a residual that is pure architectural mismatch). Every real IMPL number in the writeup is reported only after passing the two diagnostics this project invented across Eras 1-4 for exactly this failure mode (`ens_cos`, hierarchy η²), a rare case of a project's own retrospective methodology becoming a citable, load-bearing instrument three weeks later rather than just a lesson learned. Second, a pre-registered expectation was falsified in an informative way, in keeping with the month's habit of treating "it didn't reproduce as expected" as the finding: the wake-sleep loop was expected to be *necessary* for the introspective advantage, and it is not — the open-loop condition shows the same dissociation, merely weaker. A follow-up estimator correction (the main run's pooled-position η² undercounted deep structure by ~13x relative to the reference last-token estimator used everywhere else in the project) sharpened this into a cleaner claim than the one first drafted: residual DGP-structure is essentially *equal* between closed- and open-loop, so **closing the loop does not change what there is to know about the model, only how well the model comes to know it** — a distinction (know-what vs. know-how-well) with no precedent elsewhere in this history, and one worth watching for whether it recurs the way "moving frontier" did.

The arc is explicit about its own limits: `O_act`, meant to be a validated upper bound on third-party performance, undershot the self-report and the weaker `O_io` observer alike, so the claim currently rests on the ladder's flatness rather than on a demonstrated ceiling; the channel-ablation test (Test 2) turned out too blunt an instrument to carry interpretive weight; and everything is a single seed, single rule-set, single DGP regime. These are logged as open threads in the sub-README, not glossed over.

## Where it stands now (07-24)

No commits have touched `experiments/rhm/` since the confabulation writeup landed (07-23); this is the most recent completed work in the directory. The arc's own next steps — a real ceiling for the observer ladder, a token-level (rather than probe-level) report channel, and the generalization test of whether the observer ladder stays flat on a diffuse, non-hierarchical residual like natural language's — are the most concrete near-term openings, listed in full in [`confabulation/README.md`](confabulation/README.md#next-steps). More broadly, Era 7's still-untested reframe (what would let RHM's frontier move on its own, the way a human's does, without more data or privileged labels) remains the largest unresolved question standing over the whole project.

## Threads that run through the whole thing

A few observations sit above any single arc:

- **RHM's recurring role is differential diagnosis, not discovery for its own sake.** Nearly every major arc begins as an attempt to reproduce a result from the sibling `a2a_forward/` project (the wake-sleep ratchet, self-knowledge, arity/internalization) or from an idea document (confabulation), and nearly every one of them *initially fails to reproduce cleanly* on RHM, or reproduces only under conditions the project had to discover the hard way. The consistent, hard-won lesson is to treat that failure or fragility as data: the mismatch always turns out to name a hidden precondition the other domain satisfied "for free," or a regime this project had to earn — a moving frontier (ratchet), a latent rather than token target (self-knowledge), act≠plan rather than act≈plan (internalization), a sufficient rather than lossy channel (latent-vs-token planning), shared rather than domain-specific deep structure (specialization), and a small-enough forward model that the residual means something at all (confabulation, reprising Era 1 by name).

- **"Compounding requires a moving frontier" was discovered three separate times**, in three different vocabularies, months apart within the same project (the ratchet arc's retrospective in June; the latent-loop's "two novelties — sample-novelty refills breadth, only abstraction-novelty moves depth" in July; the sculpting-continual "weak ratchet, frontier exhausts in one pass" a week later). Each rediscovery sharpens rather than merely repeats the last. This is the closest thing the project has to a settled law, and the specialization arc's late reframe is best read as the first serious attempt to ask what could make that frontier move *without* being handed more data or privileged labels from outside.

- **Beliefs lag the frontier of experimentation.** The one crystallized belief document for this whole experiment (`beliefs/trees/rhm_compositional_learnability.md`) was last updated 2026-06-29 — before the KFW pivot, before latent-loop self-knowledge, before any of the control/planning/specialization/introspection work. Everything from Era 4 onward is explicitly marked in its own docs as "discussion, not yet crystallized." This isn't neglect so much as the natural lag of a fast-moving month; it does mean a large fraction of this project's most interesting, best-earned conclusions currently exist only as auxiliary READMEs, not as standing beliefs the rest of the research program can build on without re-reading them.

- **The theoretical grounding moment (Era 4) is the hinge of the whole history.** Before it, interventions read as somewhat ad hoc engineering against a stubborn empirical wall (loss reweighting, waists, chunking). After it, the same wall is understood as an instance of a named, published sample-complexity result, and essentially every subsequent arc — latent-loop, the belief-quality stage of sculpting, specialization's direct-vs-reweighted target distinction, and even confabulation's choice of substrate — is organized around the same single lever the paper names: *what you train a model to predict, not how you build it.*

- **The project's oldest methodological caution became its newest load-bearing instrument.** Era 1 logged "not testable at this scale" as a disappointing dead end for a single experiment. By Era 8, the same failure mode (an over-capacity forward model manufacturing a structureless residual) is explicitly anticipated, deliberately reproduced as a smoke-test control, shown to fabricate an entire false headline result on demand, and then guarded against with instruments (`ens_cos`, hierarchy η²) invented specifically because of it. Watching a project's own early scar turn into its most rigorously-applied piece of methodology, three-plus weeks and seven arcs later, is itself informative about how this research program accumulates — not as a list of findings, but as a growing set of things it now knows to check before believing a result.
