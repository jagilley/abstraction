# History — `experiments/mjc`

**Scope**: the MuJoCo control-substrate node. Written fresh from the READMEs and git log; supersedes any prior HISTORY.md. Full detail lives in [`README.md`](README.md) (the maintained per-node synthesis) — this is the *shape* of how we got there.

## Why this substrate exists

Born 2026-07-16 from [`ideas/physical_control_substrate.md`](../../ideas/physical_control_substrate.md), prompted by asking where a "vanilla" scaling+imitation robot policy is *structurally* confounded. The founding decision was a fork, stated explicitly and never relitigated: MuJoCo as a **controllable-dynamics DGP** (physics knobs swept like RHM's `(v,s,L,m)`, cheap policies, always a knob-sweep-vs-baseline) versus **RL-to-SOTA** (PPO-maxxing a leaderboard number). Every cut since has been graded against that fork, and the discipline shows up as a structural feature of the codebase: every perturbation knob (`puck_field`, `push_rot`, `rot_regions`, `curl_field`, …) is additive and off by default, so nothing added since 07-16 has ever broken reproducibility of what came before.

## Epoch 1 — three foundational ports, one afternoon (07-16)

[Cuts #1–#3](README.md#the-cuts-foundations) transplanted three a2a/RHM claims onto real contact physics in a single day: the FM residual concentrates at contact and turns out to be an **event detector, not a state detector** ([`contact_residual/`](contact_residual/README.md)); arity beats resolution under i.i.d. commands, the command slot is not a capacity problem ([`arity_torque/`](arity_torque/README.md)); and, uniquely enabled by a *physical* dynamics operator, intervening on the transition function while holding the task fixed shows a model-based agent re-adapts from ~50 reward-free transitions while a model-free policy needs ~240× more reward-*labeled* data ([`dynamics_shift/`](dynamics_shift/README.md)). Cut #3's incidental design choice — the planner commits to `replan_every`-step open-loop segments rather than re-planning every step — turned out to be the seed of everything that follows: its biological reading (`replan_every` ≈ how ballistic a movement is) is the thread the whole back half of the node pulls on.

## Epoch 2 — verifying the value→FM interface atom by atom, then abandoning that method (07-18)

[`value_shaping/`](value_shaping/README.md) isolated the *learning-layer* claim (a value signal re-allocates a capacity-limited FM away from irrelevant dynamics) as a stationary control, cleanly, single-loop. [`directed_readapt/`](directed_readapt/README.md) then asked whether a *value-directed* exploration drive re-adapts faster than undirected collection, and got two negatives that turned out more valuable than a positive would have been: a global shift has no scarcity for a drive to exploit (null), and a localized shift exposes that disagreement-based drives are **blind to confidently-wrong regions** — every ensemble member warm-starts from the same stale prior and agrees on the wrong answer. The composed lesson (a drive needs *scarcity* and *a signal that flags confident-wrongness*, not just novelty) is a small result with a large consequence: the node explicitly retired "verify each mechanistic atom in isolation" as a method here, in favor of "build the actual compounding phenomenon first, back-translate mechanism after" — the pivot that shapes every arc from here on.

## Epoch 3 — meta-learning over a distribution of dynamics, and the capacity-competition boundary (07-18/19)

[`meta_adapt/`](meta_adapt/README.md) (#4–#4e) reproduced RHM's own collapse — a 1-D damping family is so cross-task-shared that meta-learning buys nothing over pooling — then found the gap **opens monotonically** under a genuinely conflicting family (an actuator-rotation angle Φ, where task φ and φ+π are opposite command→motion maps). This is the epoch's first sighting of a motif that recurs for the rest of the node: value-shaping / capacity re-allocation only converts to a *control* benefit when the value-irrelevant subsystem genuinely **competes for capacity** the value-relevant one needs (#4d/#4e closed this end-to-end, including causally — a reward-driven outer loop with no knowledge of "the puck is irrelevant" rediscovers the value's support on its own, provided the budget is capacity-normalized against a scale-gaming shortcut). Absent that competition (an easy pusher, ample capacity), the same re-allocation is real on the FM side and **control-neutral** — a matched null, not a failure.

## Epoch 4 — chasing the fully-online loop, and discovering value is slow (07-20/21)

[`curiosity_control/`](curiosity_control/README.md) landed the *afferent* half (an intrinsic drive tracks a moving frontier and is specifically a non-stationarity effect) and its own cautionary finding — disagreement-style curiosity falls for a noisy TV on low-dim control (the opposite of the active-vision result), fixed only by **grounding**: explore and exploit as two additive drives, not a smarter filter. [`online_value_loop/`](online_value_loop/README.md) then tried to make both taps self-tune online from control reward, and both attempts were obstructed — for two different, informative reasons: CEM-MPC replanning is *robust* to the afferent drive's tracking effect (a **near-blind grader**), and the efferent capacity re-allocation is a slow, commit-dependent property of *training* that a fast local reward gradient structurally cannot see. This reframes as a positive result rather than a failure: **value is a slow/committed/offline-estimable quantity**, and its benefit is adaptation *speed*, not converged competence — which matters because under perpetual drift you never converge, so the "washout" is never reached in practice. [`drift_value_loop/`](drift_value_loop/README.md) then found the compounding the idea doc predicted *is* real, but comes from the fast context-latent **memory**, not the slow value-carving (which is capacity-gated and control-robust even under drift) — and diagnosed the meta-layer's apparent inertness as a **teacher problem**: grading by control (blind) versus by the value-relevant FM prediction error (the literal cerebellum→VTA messenger) is the difference between a flat landscape and a clean interior optimum.

## Epoch 5 — the ballistic resolution: it was the control mode, not the value (07-22)

[`ballistic/`](ballistic/README.md) resolves the blind-grader puzzle definitively: a **ballistic** (feedforward, committed) controller — the biologically necessary regime, since you cannot replan faster than sensorimotor delay — makes FM quality ~3× more behaviorally load-bearing than a reactive one, and reward-free re-adaptation restores ballistic competence ~4.3× more than reactive after a drift. The synthesis (the "reward-free / ballistic synergy") is a load-bearing biological reading that the node now treats as settled: the cerebellar forward model is simultaneously the one motor asset **maintainable without reward** and the one that **feedforward control depends on** — the same object, which is presented as *why* evolution built it this way rather than as a coincidence.

## Epoch 6 — directed collection, a retracted null, and the discipline of auditing your own instrument (07-22)

[`ballistic/directed/`](ballistic/directed/README.md) built the last missing link (does the value loop's choice of *where to collect* pay?) and initially reported a real-looking null in the online-loop configuration (S2). A later self-audit retracted it: the metric was a control grader this node had already proven blind, 62% contaminated by post-recovery plateau, and the aggregate was flipped by one seed's floating-point degenerate-allocation catastrophe — on top of which every arm was quietly subsidized by **2,240 free, globally-teleported probe/monitor transitions per round against a 100-transition budget** (a 22× subsidy that demotes "where should I look" to a tiebreaker). The one-shot version (S1) survived audit untouched and is a clean positive. The retraction is treated as a first-class result in its own right — the write-up documents *why* the mistake was made (reading a belief downgrade off an instrument already published as blind) as the transferable content, not just the corrected number.

## Epoch 7 — making acquisition itself a DGP axis (07-23/24)

The S2 subsidy names the fix: collection had always been free teleportation. [`on_policy/`](on_policy/README.md) builds embodied collection (`Body`, no `set_state`, every step metered) as a flag, gated bit-identical against every prior teleport-based cut. The pricing (E0–E2) delivers two corrections to the node's own prior history: most of on-policy's apparent sample-efficiency advantage was actually a **mistuned teleport sampling knob**, not embodiment (an oracle teleporter closes the gap); and the one property that *is* structurally embodied — command-state entanglement from continuity (|corr(u,s)| ≈ 0.7) — quietly means Cut #2's founding i.i.d.-command premise was a teleport-only luxury a real body never gets. Embodiment's cost turns out conditional on drift structure: nearly free when global (E1), a real 2.5× tax when local, caused by bootstrap data-quality rather than coverage (E2). [`on_policy/directed_on_policy/`](on_policy/directed_on_policy/README.md) (E3) then re-runs the retracted S2 loop where the subsidy structurally cannot exist, and the retracted claim comes back positive: relevance and reducibility both pay once looking is embodied.

## Epoch 8 — the flagship question finally run, and partly retired (07-27)

[`expansion/`](expansion/README.md) (Cut #5) answers the question the founding idea doc called "the single biggest open question of the whole program," left unrun for months: does drifting a plant's physics open the forward model's representational frontier? Built as calibrate→measure→calibrate specifically because rank-shaped instruments had failed three times elsewhere in the repo, the answer is a clean, calibrated no — drift moves the *target function* by ±0.08 directions against a **known-real** +0.72±0.42 calibration effect from an actual support increase, ~10× smaller. This is written back into [`ideas/physical_control_substrate.md`](../../ideas/physical_control_substrate.md) as an explicit retraction of that document's "drift is a free novelty generator" framing (not of drift's value for realism, which survives) — a rare case in this node of a founding motivating claim being corrected on its own terms after the infrastructure built to test it was already mature.

## Epoch 9 — a second body, and what transplants across substrates (07-22 onward)

[`arm_substrate/`](arm_substrate/README.md) was built to retire the pusher's two structural debts: every cut's "single family" caveat, and capacity competition that had to be *manufactured* (multi-mode force fields) rather than being intrinsic to the plant. It independently re-derives nearly every major finding (the value-relevant-teacher correction, the smooth-not-localized quality axis, the capacity-competition boundary as a literal mass in kilograms) and buys one readout the pusher structurally cannot produce — a mirror-signed adaptation aftereffect, the clearest single piece of evidence that re-adaptation is a genuine model update. [`on_policy/metered_repair/`](on_policy/metered_repair/README.md) (E4/E5, 07-29–07-31) then imports two claims from the RHM sculpting line onto this substrate: the diagnosis that a fixed-budget "learning progress" tap is estimator-inverted **ports exactly**, but repairing it does not cash out into a better outcome here — except for one clean new result (relevance and reducibility are only *both* necessary once a visited-but-irreducible region is deliberately placed, a geometry neither substrate had before). The necessity question (does metered data force representation to migrate to a shared, deeper level) returns a clean negative with a mechanical cause: spatially-disjoint local fields give an MLP no route to shared-parameter inference at all, sharpening rather than contradicting Epoch 8's finding that this plant has no hierarchy to expand into.

## Throughlines — what the node now believes that it didn't in July

- **A stale/wrong FM is often invisible to the controller that would reveal it.** Replanning control absorbs FM-quality differences by re-grounding every step; several apparent nulls across epochs 4–7 turned out to be this, not the phenomenon's absence, and the fix (grade by the value-relevant FM prediction error, not downstream control) recurs from [`drift_value_loop`](drift_value_loop/README.md) through [`on_policy/directed_on_policy`](on_policy/directed_on_policy/README.md).
- **Free/omniscient measurement quietly manufactures results.** Two separate epochs (6, 7) trace an apparent scientific effect back to an uncharged teleportation subsidy. The standing fix is structural (a metered `Body` that cannot expose `set_state`), not a discipline to remember.
- **Re-allocation and directed collection are boundary-condition phenomena, not always-on ones.** They pay specifically under capacity competition / genuine scarcity, and are neutral-to-null otherwise — true in [`value_shaping`](value_shaping/README.md), [`meta_adapt`](meta_adapt/README.md) #4d/e, and [`drift_value_loop`](drift_value_loop/README.md) Cut 2 alike.
- **Novelty (drift) and expansion (growing representable directions) are not the same axis** — settled in Epoch 8, and now understood to require a domain with actual hierarchy, which a motor plant structurally is not.
